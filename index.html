Petra is one of the New7Wonders of the World and a UNESCO World Heritage Site located in southern Jordan. Carved directly into vibrant rose-colored sandstone cliffs over 2,000 years ago by the Nabataeans, the city was once a thriving trade hub connecting Arabia, Egypt, and the Mediterranean. Today, Petra is one of the most photographed archaeological sites on Earth. Visitors enter through The Siq, a narrow gorge stretching about 1.2 km before opening dramatically onto the Treasury façade. The site sits at roughly 950 meters above sea level, and some tombs date back to the 1st century BCE. The old entrance fee of 25 JD has been updated to 50 JD for foreign visitors as of the latest tourism regulations.

Petra is best visited between March and May or September and November, when temperatures are mild.
